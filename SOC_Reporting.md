# SOC Reporting

## 1. Daily Reports
Daily reports provide a snapshot of security activities and incidents that occurred within the last 24 hours.  
They include details such as the number of alerts processed, incidents investigated, and any immediate actions taken.  
Daily reporting ensures that SOC teams maintain operational awareness and that management can track ongoing issues in real time.  
These reports are concise but comprehensive, allowing quick review of the organization’s current security posture.

---

## 2. Weekly Reports
Weekly reports summarize security operations over a longer period, typically seven days.  
They highlight trends, recurring issues, and patterns in alerts or incidents.  
Weekly reporting provides context that daily reports may not capture, such as increases in phishing attempts or repeated login failures across departments.  
These reports are useful for identifying systemic problems and planning improvements in detection and response strategies.

---

## 3. Incident Summaries
Incident summaries document the details of specific security incidents from detection to resolution.  
They include information such as the timeline of events, affected systems, root cause analysis, and remediation steps taken.  
Incident summaries serve as a record for future reference, helping SOC teams learn from past experiences and refine their processes.  
They also provide evidence for compliance audits and demonstrate accountability in incident handling.

---

## 4. Threat Reports
Threat reports focus on documenting and analyzing external and internal threats observed during monitoring.  
They describe attack vectors, adversary tactics, and the potential impact on the organization.  
Threat reports often incorporate intelligence feeds to provide context about emerging risks and attacker behavior.  
These reports help SOC teams anticipate future attacks and strengthen defenses by adapting detection rules and response strategies.

---

## 5. Executive Communication
Executive communication translates technical security findings into business‑level insights for leadership.  
It avoids technical jargon and emphasizes the strategic impact of incidents, such as financial risk, regulatory exposure, or reputational damage.  
Executive communication ensures that decision‑makers understand the organization’s risk posture and can allocate resources effectively.  
By presenting security as a measurable business function, SOC teams gain support for investments in tools, staffing, and training.

---

## Summary
SOC reporting provides structured communication at multiple levels.  
Daily and weekly reports maintain operational visibility, incident summaries document detailed case handling, threat reports highlight adversary activity, and executive communication connects security outcomes to business priorities.  
Together, these reporting practices ensure that every stakeholder, from analysts to executives, has the information needed to maintain strong and resilient security operations.
