# SOC Metrics

## 1. Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)
Mean Time to Detect (MTTD) is the average amount of time it takes for the SOC team to identify that a security incident has occurred.  
It measures the speed of detection from the moment the attack begins until the moment it is noticed by monitoring systems or analysts.  
A lower MTTD indicates that the SOC is effective at spotting threats quickly, which reduces the window of opportunity for attackers to cause damage.  
Improving MTTD requires strong visibility, well‑tuned detection rules, and proactive monitoring practices.

---

## 2. Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)
Mean Time to Respond (MTTR) is the average amount of time it takes for the SOC team to take corrective action after detecting an incident.  
This includes containment, eradication, and recovery steps.  
A shorter MTTR demonstrates that the SOC can act swiftly to minimize the impact of a breach.  
Reducing MTTR depends on clear workflows, automation tools, and effective communication between analysts and stakeholders.

---

## 3. Incident Volume
Incident volume refers to the total number of security incidents handled by the SOC within a given period.  
Tracking incident volume helps organizations understand the scale of threats they face and the workload of their analysts.  
High incident volume may indicate frequent attacks, misconfigured detection rules, or an expanding attack surface.  
Managing incident volume effectively requires prioritization, automation, and continuous improvement of detection mechanisms.

---

## 4. Detection Effectiveness
Detection effectiveness measures how accurately the SOC identifies genuine threats compared to false positives or missed incidents.  
It reflects the quality of detection rules, the precision of monitoring tools, and the skill of analysts in interpreting alerts.  
High detection effectiveness means that most alerts correspond to real threats, reducing wasted effort and ensuring that critical incidents are not overlooked.  
Improving detection effectiveness involves refining rules, integrating threat intelligence, and conducting regular tuning of monitoring systems.

---

## 5. Operational KPIs
Operational Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) are measurable values that indicate how well the SOC is performing overall.  
These may include metrics such as analyst productivity, escalation rates, incident closure times, and compliance with service level agreements.  
Operational KPIs provide management with insights into efficiency, resource allocation, and areas needing improvement.  
By tracking KPIs consistently, organizations can evaluate SOC maturity, justify investments, and ensure that security operations align with business objectives.

---

## Summary
SOC metrics such as MTTD, MTTR, incident volume, detection effectiveness, and operational KPIs provide a structured way to measure the performance of security operations.  
They highlight how quickly threats are detected, how effectively they are resolved, and how well the SOC team manages its workload.  
Together, these metrics guide continuous improvement, strengthen defenses, and ensure that the SOC delivers reliable protection against evolving cyber threats.
