# Alert Management

## 1. Alert Triage
Alert triage is the process of reviewing incoming alerts to determine their relevance and severity.  
It involves examining the source of the alert, the type of activity detected, and the potential impact on the organization.  
Through triage, analysts separate genuine security concerns from routine or low‑risk events.  
This step ensures that valuable time is spent on alerts that truly require investigation rather than being wasted on noise.

---

## 2. Alert Prioritization
Alert prioritization refers to ranking alerts based on their urgency and potential damage.  
High‑priority alerts may involve critical systems, sensitive data, or indicators of active compromise.  
Lower‑priority alerts may represent minor anomalies or policy violations with limited impact.  
Prioritization helps SOC teams allocate resources effectively, ensuring that the most dangerous threats are addressed first while still keeping track of less urgent issues.

---

## 3. False Positives
False positives are alerts that appear suspicious but are later proven to be harmless.  
They often occur due to overly sensitive detection rules, misconfigured monitoring tools, or normal user behavior being flagged incorrectly.  
Managing false positives is essential because they consume analyst time and can distract from genuine threats.  
SOC teams refine detection rules, adjust thresholds, and apply contextual analysis to reduce the number of false positives and improve overall efficiency.

---

## 4. Alert Escalation
Alert escalation is the process of transferring an alert to a higher‑tier analyst or specialized team when deeper expertise is required.  
For example, a Level 1 analyst may escalate an alert to Level 2 if advanced investigation or containment actions are needed.  
Escalation ensures that complex incidents are handled by personnel with the right skills and authority.  
It also creates a structured chain of responsibility, preventing delays and ensuring that critical threats are managed appropriately.

---

## 5. Investigation Workflows
Investigation workflows describe the systematic steps analysts follow when examining alerts.  
This typically includes gathering evidence from logs, correlating events across systems, identifying the root cause, and assessing the scope of impact.  
Workflows provide consistency by ensuring that every investigation follows a repeatable process.  
They also support documentation, making it easier to share findings, learn from incidents, and improve detection rules for future monitoring.

---

## Summary
Alert management is a structured discipline that begins with triage, continues with prioritization, and requires careful handling of false positives.  
Escalation ensures that complex cases reach the right experts, while investigation workflows provide a clear path to uncovering the truth behind suspicious activity.  
Together, these practices enable SOC teams to respond quickly, reduce wasted effort, and strengthen the organization’s defense against cyber threats.
