# SOC Escalation Processes

## 1. Tier 1 Operations
Tier 1 operations are the frontline activities performed by entry‑level SOC analysts.  
Their responsibilities include monitoring alerts, performing initial triage, and identifying whether an event is a false positive or requires deeper investigation.  
Tier 1 analysts focus on speed and accuracy, ensuring that suspicious activity is quickly flagged and documented.  
They act as the first layer of defense, filtering noise and escalating genuine threats to higher tiers when necessary.

---

## 2. Tier 2 Investigations
Tier 2 investigations are handled by more experienced analysts who take over when alerts require advanced analysis.  
These analysts perform deeper log correlation, forensic review, and threat validation.  
They investigate the root cause of incidents, assess the scope of impact, and determine whether containment or remediation is needed.  
Tier 2 analysts provide expertise that goes beyond basic triage, ensuring that complex threats are properly understood and managed.

---

## 3. Escalation Paths
Escalation paths define the structured routes through which alerts move from one tier to another.  
They ensure that incidents requiring specialized knowledge or authority are passed to the right team without delay.  
For example, a suspicious login detected by Tier 1 may be escalated to Tier 2 for investigation, and if confirmed as a breach, further escalated to incident response or management teams.  
Clear escalation paths prevent confusion, reduce response time, and guarantee accountability at every stage.

---

## 4. Communication Workflows
Communication workflows describe how information is shared during escalation.  
They include notifying relevant stakeholders, updating incident tickets, and maintaining clear records of actions taken.  
Effective communication ensures that all parties involved understand the incident status, responsibilities, and next steps.  
Without structured workflows, critical details may be lost, leading to delays or mismanagement of incidents.

---

## 5. Incident Ownership
Incident ownership refers to assigning responsibility for managing an incident from detection to resolution.  
The owner is accountable for coordinating investigation, escalation, and remediation efforts.  
Ownership ensures that incidents are not overlooked or abandoned, and that progress is tracked until closure.  
By defining clear ownership, SOC teams maintain accountability and ensure that every incident receives the attention it requires.

---

## Summary
SOC escalation processes rely on structured tiered operations, defined escalation paths, and clear communication workflows.  
Tier 1 analysts handle initial triage, Tier 2 analysts perform deeper investigations, and escalation ensures that incidents reach the right expertise level.  
Incident ownership guarantees accountability, while communication workflows keep all stakeholders informed.  
Together, these processes create a disciplined approach to managing security incidents efficiently and effectively.
